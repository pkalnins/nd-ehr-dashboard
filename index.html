Study Abstract

Background. Electronic health record (EHR) data from community-based naturopathic clinics represent a substantial but underutilized resource for health services research. Prior studies have relied on billing and administrative data, which do not capture the clinical narrative — treatment plans, lifestyle counseling, and supplement recommendations — that characterize naturopathic practice.

Objectives. To develop and validate a privacy-first, cloud-based informatics infrastructure that transforms multi-clinic naturopathic EHR data into a standardized, de-identified, structured research dataset, and to demonstrate its analytic utility through an HbA1c-defined glycemic cohort analysis.

Methods. Data were exported via FHIR API from seven ChARM EHR-based naturopathic clinics in Washington state. De-identification was performed using the Google Cloud Healthcare API, supplemented by a custom 9-pass rule-based NLP redaction pipeline verified by manual safety audit. The resulting dataset was loaded into BigQuery. De-identified clinical notes were converted into structured, queryable data: SOAP sections were parsed programmatically and curated keyword dictionaries applied to clinician-authored sections to classify treatment mentions into 13 standardized categories. A glycemic cohort was defined by elevated HbA1c (≥5.7%). Longitudinal HbA1c change was assessed by paired t-test (first vs. last reading). Rule-based NLP was validated by manual clinician review (PPV 97.9%).

Results. The database includes 58,498 patients, 469,162 encounters, 858,394 clinical notes, and 3.6 million observations across 7 clinics. Among 2,869 patients with ≥2 HbA1c readings, longitudinal analysis stratified by baseline glycemic category showed mean changes of −0.686% (diabetes, n=128, p<0.001), −0.139% (prediabetes, n=783, p<0.001), and −0.028% (normoglycemic at baseline, n=1,958, p<0.001). Supplement/botanical recommendations were documented for 83.4% of the elevated-HbA1c cohort; diet/nutrition counseling for 58.6%; and exercise counseling for 50.9%. Diet/nutrition and stress/mindfulness counseling were more frequent in the diabetes group than in prediabetes or normoglycemic patients.

Conclusions. A scalable, HIPAA-compliant cloud pipeline successfully transformed multi-clinic naturopathic EHR data into a structured research dataset. The glycemic cohort analysis demonstrates both the database’s analytic utility and clinically meaningful glycemic improvement in patients receiving naturopathic care, with a treatment pattern consistent with prior survey-based characterizations of naturopathic practice.
